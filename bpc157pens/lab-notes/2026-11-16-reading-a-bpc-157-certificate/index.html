What to read on a BPC-157 certificate

A useful BPC-157 certificate names the compound, shows a lot number that matches the label, reports HPLC purity, and confirms identity by mass spectrometry. The molecular weight used for that identity check is about 1419.5 g/mol. The certificate is a test record for a lot. It is not an FDA approval and it is not permission for human use.

By the bpc157pens.com Editorial Team · November 16, 2026

What the page should name

Start with the compound name and the lot. If the lot on the certificate is not the lot on the vial or pen, the document is about a different container. Then read the purity method. For this peptide the expected method is HPLC. Identity belongs in a mass result near 1419.5 g/mol, the figure PubChem lists for BPC-157 (CAS 137525-51-0).

The testing laboratory and the test date belong on the same page. A certificate with no lab and no date cannot be checked later. Palm Beach Vitality says lot-specific certificates are available on request. That is a seller statement about access to the document, not a claim that every lot has been reviewed on this site.

What the page does not do

Purity is a measurement of the material that was tested. It does not say the peptide is approved, that a human study found a benefit, or that the next lot will match this one. BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. A clean chromatogram does not change that.

This note stops at how to read the document. It does not say how much material to take out of a pen or a vial.
