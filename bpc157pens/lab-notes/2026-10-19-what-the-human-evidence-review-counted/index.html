What the human-evidence review counted

A 2026 systematic review found three small human studies of BPC-157, 30 participants in total, and rated the certainty of that evidence as very low. Most of the published record is cell and animal work. The review’s authors did not write, review, or endorse this site.

By the bpc157pens.com Editorial Team · October 19, 2026

The count

Poolton, Minett, and Kazmi looked for human evidence on BPC-157 in orthopaedic and regenerative medicine. The yield was three small studies. Thirty people is not a base for a use claim, and the reviewers said so by rating certainty very low. A second 2025 review, by Vasireddi and colleagues, is the other overview this site keeps on the research page. It is also a review of published papers, not a trial of the pens sold here.

Cell and animal papers can describe a mechanism or a laboratory model. They are not human results. This site’s research guide separates those layers on purpose.

What this note will not add

Naming a paper is not a finding that the peptide “works.” The authors studied what they studied. They are not a product endorsement. Nothing here is a dose, a protocol, or a reason to use a research chemical in a person or an animal outside a legitimate laboratory.

Sources
