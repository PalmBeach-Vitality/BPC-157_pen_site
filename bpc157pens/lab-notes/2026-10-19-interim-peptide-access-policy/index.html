Interim peptide access policy

INDUSTRY UPDATE

This week covers October 12 through October 19. On October 14, HHS and FDA submitted an Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides to the White House Office of Management and Budget. The public listing is RIN 0910-ZD94, a pending notice. It names no compound, and the policy has not been issued.

Must-know

The Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs received the notice on October 14. The listing shows no legal deadline and marks the notice not economically significant. Holland & Knight reported the filing on October 16. Reuters reported it on October 15, and POLITICO Prescription Pulse reported it on October 16. The text is not on the public listing.

Reporting that cites unnamed sources describes the expected approach as temporary enforcement discretion: state-licensed 503A pharmacies might compound certain peptides under a valid prescription while FDA moves toward a 503A Bulks List rule. Holland & Knight states that formal rulemaking on the July advisory votes has not begun. The notice does not identify which peptides would be covered. The six peptides the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee recommended in July are BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, and Epitalon. Those votes sit outside this window.

That kind of enforcement discretion would not approve any peptide and would not place a substance on the final Bulks List. Holland & Knight states it would not cover research-use-only products, supplements, or 503B outsourcing facilities, and that it could be withdrawn without formal rulemaking. This remains a procedural step. The statute is unchanged. Research-use-only products remain unapproved new drugs when intended for human use.

PRODUCT SPOTLIGHT

BPC-157

BPC-157 is the compound people pick when the interest is tissue repair. It is a synthetic 15-amino-acid fragment of a protective protein in human gastric juice, and the work around it sits on tendon, ligament, muscle, and the gut lining. Muscle-healing research (PubMed 30915550), a wound-healing review (PubMed 34267654), the 2025 orthopaedic sports-medicine review (PubMed 40756949), and the healing review across tendon, ligament, muscle, and bone (PubMed 29998800) are why it stays in that spot. Preclinical tendon models have reported faster collagen organization. Muscle-injury models have associated it with functional recovery markers and angiogenesis-related histology. Gut models are where the cytoprotection story started. Someone researching soft-tissue recovery, blood-vessel formation in injured tissue, or gut-lining protection is looking at this compound for that reason. ALSO ON THE RADAR

Sterility recalls in compounding

Recalls that started in September are still being reported. MedShadow on October 16, and Karmactive on October 17, describe FDA enforcement reports for compounded injectable lots from two licensed 503A pharmacies. Carolina Infusion, in Ridgeland, South Carolina, initiated a Class II recall on September 10 for lack of sterility assurance tied to bacteriostatic water and processing equipment. The lots include sermorelin acetate, semaglutide, tirzepatide, and related vitamin combinations. Houston RX, in Houston, initiated Class II recalls on September 25: sermorelin for lack of process control, and tirzepatide with niacinamide for lack of sterility assurance. These are quality findings inside the licensed compounding channel. They are not a new shortage action, and they are not a change in peptide policy. They do not change the status of research-use-only material.
