What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound 157) is a synthetic peptide made of 15 amino acids. It is modeled on a fragment of a larger protein found in human gastric juice and was first described by a research group at the University of Zagreb in Croatia.2

What has lab research studied about BPC-157?

Published BPC-157 research is almost entirely in cell cultures and animals, mostly rats. Studies have looked at tissue-repair models, blood-vessel growth, cell migration and how the body breaks the peptide down. These are laboratory findings, not evidence of effects in people.

A large share of the animal research comes from one research group, led by Predrag Sikirić at the University of Zagreb.2 Independent replication is limited, which is one reason reviewers rate the overall evidence as early-stage.

How strong is the evidence for BPC-157?

Weak, for humans. A 2025 systematic review found 36 relevant studies, of which 35 were preclinical and one was clinical.8 A 2026 systematic review found three human studies with 30 participants in total and rated the certainty of that evidence as very low.9

  1. Cell studies Many. Show what the peptide does to cells in a dish.
  2. Animal studies Most of the literature. Largely rats, largely one research group.
  3. Human studies Three small, uncontrolled reports: a 16-person retrospective chart review, a 12-person pilot, and a 2-person safety pilot.10–12
  4. Controlled human trials None published.

The 2026 review judged all three human studies to be of poor methodological quality and concluded that BPC-157 should not be used in people outside ethically approved, regulated clinical trials.9 The 2025 review found no clinical safety data.8

How should BPC-157 pens and vials be stored?

Keep BPC-157 pens and liquid vials refrigerated at 2–8 °C (36–46 °F), protected from light, and never frozen. The seller lists a practical shelf life of 4–6 weeks for pens and 16–20 weeks for liquid vials.20

  • Temperature: 2–8 °C (36–46 °F). Avoid long periods at room temperature.
  • Freezing: do not freeze either liquid format.
  • Light and moisture: store in the original packaging, away from light.
  • Discard: after the seller’s shelf-life window, or sooner if the solution turns cloudy, discolored, or shows particles.
  • Records: note the lot number and the date the pen or vial was first opened.

What should a BPC-157 certificate of analysis (COA) show?

A useful BPC-157 COA ties a specific lot to lab results: a lot number that matches the label, HPLC purity, identity confirmed by mass spectrometry, and the testing lab and date. Palm Beach Vitality provides lot-specific COAs on request.

What to look for

  • Lot number that matches the number on your pen or vial.
  • HPLC purity, reported as a percentage.
  • Mass spectrometry identity consistent with BPC-157’s molecular weight of about 1419.5 g/mol.1
  • Testing lab name and the date of the test.
  • Compound name and sequence, matching GEPPPGKPADDAGLV.

Red flags in a supplier

  • Health or healing claims for a research-use-only product.
  • Dosing charts, “protocols” or usage instructions.
  • Before-and-after photos or testimonials.
  • A COA with no lot number, no lab name or no date.
  • One generic COA used for every batch.

Is BPC-157 legal in the U.S.? Regulatory status as of November 2026

BPC-157 is not FDA-approved for any use. It is sold in the U.S. labeled for laboratory research use only. In 2023 FDA flagged it as a compounding substance that may present significant safety risks; in July 2026 an FDA advisory committee voted 8–6 to recommend reversing that, but FDA has not finalized any change.

  1. 2023

    FDA places BPC-157 among bulk drug substances for compounding that may present significant safety risks, citing possible immune reactions, peptide impurities, and limited safety information.13

  2. July 23, 2026

    FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee votes 8 to 6, with one abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157 to the list of substances compounding pharmacies may use. FDA’s own review staff had recommended against it.14,15

  3. Now

    The vote is advisory. Any change requires FDA rulemaking, which has not been completed. BPC-157 remains unapproved.14,15

Sport and military

The World Anti-Doping Agency lists BPC-157 under S0, non-approved substances, prohibited at all times.16 The U.S. Department of Defense also lists it as prohibited for service members.17

What “research use only” means

It is a label for materials intended for laboratory work. It is not an FDA approval, and it does not make a product a drug, a dietary supplement, or suitable for human use. This page is general information, not legal advice.

What are the open safety questions about BPC-157?

The main open questions are the lack of human safety data, a theoretical concern that encouraging blood-vessel growth could also support tumor growth, and the quality of products sold online.8,18,19

  • No clinical safety data. Reviewers found no controlled data on safety in people.8
  • Blood-vessel growth. The same pro-angiogenic effect seen in lab studies has raised a theoretical concern about tumor growth that has not been ruled in or out.6,18
  • Product quality. Peptides sold online may not contain what the label says, which is why lot-specific testing matters.19
  • Unknown long-term effects. No long-term studies exist in any species at scale.9
