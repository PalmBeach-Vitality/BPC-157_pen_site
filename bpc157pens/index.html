§1 Formats

Two ready-to-use formats. Pens first.

Every product ships with BPC-157 already in solution; no reconstitution needed. A pen keeps it sealed in a 3 mL cartridge with a built-in dial; a vial holds 10 mL and is drawn with separate lab tools.

§2 The molecule

Fifteen amino acids, one at a time.

In the lab, BPC-157 is built one amino acid at a time on a microscopic resin bead, starting from valine and ending with glycine. Each new link releases one molecule of water.

§6 U.S. status

Still unapproved.

An FDA advisory committee voted in July 2026 to recommend allowing BPC-157 in compounding. FDA has not acted on that vote.

  1. 2023

    FDA lists BPC-157 among compounding substances that may present significant safety risks.

  2. July 23, 2026

    FDA’s compounding advisory committee votes 8–6, with one abstention, to recommend reversing that.

  3. Today

    The vote is advisory. No rule has been finalized, and BPC-157 remains unapproved.

§7 FAQ

Common questions.

Short answers to the questions researchers ask most. The full list has twelve.

All questions
Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. As of November 15, 2026, BPC-157 is not approved by the FDA for any human or veterinary use.

Does a BPC-157 pen need reconstitution?

No. Every BPC-157 pen and vial Palm Beach Vitality sells ships already in solution. Only freeze-dried BPC-157 needs reconstitution, and it is available for bulk orders only.

How should BPC-157 pens and vials be stored?

Refrigerate at 2–8 °C (36–46 °F), protect from light, and do not freeze.

Why doesn’t this site give dosing information?

BPC-157 is not approved for human use, and the products this site links to are sold for laboratory research only. Dosing guidance would be medical advice this site cannot give.
