What does BPC-157 stand for?

BPC-157 stands for Body Protection Compound 157. It is a synthetic peptide of 15 amino acids (a pentadecapeptide) modeled on a fragment of a protein found in human gastric juice. It is also known as PL 14736 and Bepecin. Read more →

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. As of November 15, 2026, BPC-157 is not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for any human or veterinary use. In 2023 FDA listed it among compounding substances that may present significant safety risks. A July 2026 FDA advisory committee vote recommended reversing that, but FDA has not finalized any change. Is BPC-157 legal to buy in the U.S.?

BPC-157 is sold in the U.S. labeled for laboratory research use only. That label is not an FDA approval, and it does not make BPC-157 a drug, a dietary supplement, or a product for human use. Its compounding status is under FDA review. This answer is general information, not legal advice. Read more →

What is a BPC-157 pen?

A BPC-157 pen is a pre-filled pen that holds BPC-157 already in solution in a sealed 3 mL cartridge, with a dial for dispensing measured volumes in the lab. Palm Beach Vitality's pens contain 10 mg (about 3.33 mg/mL) or 20 mg (about 6.67 mg/mL) of BPC-157. Read more →

Both formats sold by Palm Beach Vitality are already in solution. A pen is a sealed 3 mL cartridge with a built-in dial. Freeze-dried BPC-157 is powder that must be reconstituted first; Palm Beach Vitality offers it for bulk orders only. Read more →

No. Every BPC-157 product Palm Beach Vitality sells, pens and vials alike, ships already in solution, so there is no mixing or transfer step. Only freeze-dried (lyophilized) BPC-157 needs reconstitution, and Palm Beach Vitality offers it for bulk orders only. Read more →

How is BPC-157 made?

BPC-157 is made by solid-phase peptide synthesis. Amino acids are added one at a time to a chain anchored on resin beads, starting from valine and ending with glycine. The finished chain is cut from the resin, purified by HPLC, checked by mass spectrometry, then filled into pens or vials. Read more →

How should BPC-157 pens and vials be stored?

Refrigerate at 2 to 8 °C (36 to 46 °F), protect from light, and do not freeze. The seller lists a practical shelf life of 4 to 6 weeks for pens and 16 to 20 weeks for liquid vials. Discard any solution that turns cloudy, discolored, or shows particles. Read more →

What should a BPC-157 certificate of analysis (COA) show?

A useful COA names the compound, shows a lot number that matches the product label, reports HPLC purity, confirms identity by mass spectrometry (BPC-157 is about 1419.5 g/mol), and lists the testing lab and test date. Palm Beach Vitality provides lot-specific COAs on request. Read more →

Both use the same 3 mL pre-filled pen. The 20 mg pen holds twice the total peptide, so its concentration is about 6.67 mg/mL compared with about 3.33 mg/mL for the 10 mg pen. Read more →

Is BPC-157 banned in sports?

Yes. The World Anti-Doping Agency lists BPC-157 under S0, non-approved substances, so it is prohibited at all times for athletes under the World Anti-Doping Code. The U.S. Department of Defense also lists it as prohibited for service members. Read more →
