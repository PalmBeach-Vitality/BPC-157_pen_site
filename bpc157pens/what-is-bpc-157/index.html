What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound 157) is a synthetic peptide made of 15 amino acids. It is modeled on a fragment of a larger protein found in human gastric juice and was first described by a research group at the University of Zagreb in Croatia.2

By the bpc157pens.com Editorial Team · Last reviewed November 15, 2026

Sequence

G·E·P·P·P·G·K·P·A·D·D·A·G·L·V

Fifteen residues joined by fourteen peptide bonds. The four prolines are part of why researchers describe the peptide as unusually stable.

What has lab research studied?

Published BPC-157 research is almost entirely in cell cultures and animals, mostly rats. These are laboratory findings, not evidence of effects in people.

How strong is the evidence?

Weak, for humans. A 2025 systematic review found 36 relevant studies, 35 preclinical and one clinical.8 A 2026 review found three human studies with 30 participants in total and rated the certainty as very low.9

  1. Cell studiesMany. What the peptide does to cells in a dish.
  2. Animal studiesMost of the literature. Largely rats, largely one group.
  3. Human studiesThree small, uncontrolled reports: a 16-person chart review, a 12-person pilot and a 2-person safety pilot.10–12
  4. Controlled human trialsNone published.

The 2026 review judged all three human studies to be of poor quality and concluded BPC-157 should not be used in people outside regulated clinical trials.9 The 20 studies behind this summary, with a plain-language note on each →

Open safety questions

  • No clinical safety data. Reviewers found no controlled data on safety in people.8
  • Blood-vessel growth. The pro-angiogenic effect seen in lab studies raises a theoretical tumor-growth concern that has not been ruled in or out.6,13
  • Product quality. Peptides sold online may not contain what the label says, which is why lot-specific testing matters.14
