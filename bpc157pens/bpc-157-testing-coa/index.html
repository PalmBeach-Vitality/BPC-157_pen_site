Anatomy of a COA

Check five things on any BPC-157 COA: the compound and sequence, the lot number, HPLC purity, mass-spec identity, and who tested it and when.

  1. Compound and sequence. Should name BPC-157 and match GEPPPGKPADDAGLV.
  2. Lot number. Must match the number printed on your pen or vial.
  3. HPLC purity. Reported as a percentage of the total peak area.
  4. Mass-spec identity. Observed mass consistent with BPC-157’s molecular weight of about 1419.5 g/mol.1
  5. Testing lab and date. A named lab and a test date for that lot.

Reading HPLC purity

HPLC pushes a sample through a column and records each component as a peak. Purity is the main peak’s share of the total peak area. A shorter chain, or another ingredient in a blend, shows up as its own peak.

Supplier red flags

Be wary of any research peptide seller that makes health claims, gives dosing advice, or can’t produce a lot-specific COA. Products sold online may not contain what the label says.2

  • Health or healing claims for a research-use-only product.
  • Dosing charts, “protocols” or usage instructions.
  • Before-and-after photos or testimonials.
  • A COA with no lot number, no lab name or no date.
  • One generic COA used for every batch.
