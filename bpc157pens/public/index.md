## What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound 157) is a synthetic peptide made of 15 amino acids. It is modeled on a fragment of a larger protein found in human gastric juice and was first described by a research group at the University of Zagreb in Croatia. [2]

| Identifier | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Full name | Body Protection Compound 157 |
| Other names | PL 14736, Bepecin, stable gastric pentadecapeptide BPC 157 |
| Sequence | Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV) |
| Molecular formula | C62H98N16O22 |
| Molecular weight | 1419.5 g/mol |
| CAS number | 137525-51-0 |

Source: PubChem CID 9941957. [1]

## What has lab research studied about BPC-157?

Published BPC-157 research is almost entirely in cell cultures and animals, mostly rats. Studies have looked at tissue-repair models, blood-vessel growth, cell migration and how the body breaks the peptide down. These are laboratory findings, not evidence of effects in people.

| What was studied | Model | Reported finding | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tendon cell behavior | Rat tendon cells and tissue, in the lab | Faster outgrowth and migration of tendon cells, and better cell survival under stress, linked to the FAK-paxillin pathway | Chang et al., 2011 [4] |
| Growth hormone receptor | Rat tendon cells, in the lab | Increased growth hormone receptor expression in tendon cells | Chang et al., 2014 [5] |
| Blood-vessel growth | Chick embryo membrane, human endothelial cells, rats | More vessel formation, associated with VEGFR2 receptor activation | Hsieh et al., 2017 [6] |
| Wound-healing models | Rats (review of the Zagreb group's work) | Reported healing effects across many rat injury models | Seiwerth et al., 2021 [3] |
| How the body processes it | Rats and beagle dogs | Elimination half-life under 30 minutes; broken down into small fragments and amino acids | He et al., 2022 [7] |

A large share of the animal research comes from one research group, led by Predrag Sikirić at the University of Zagreb. [2] Independent replication is limited.

## How strong is the evidence for BPC-157?

Weak, for humans. A 2025 systematic review found 36 relevant studies, of which 35 were preclinical and one was clinical. [8] A 2026 systematic review found three human studies with 30 participants in total and rated the certainty of that evidence as very low. [9]

- Cell studies: many.
- Animal studies: most of the literature, largely rats, largely one research group.
- Human studies: three small, uncontrolled reports (a 16-person retrospective chart review, a 12-person pilot, and a 2-person safety pilot). [10-12]
- Controlled human trials: none published.

The 2026 review judged all three human studies to be of poor methodological quality and concluded that BPC-157 should not be used in people outside ethically approved, regulated clinical trials. [9] The 2025 review found no clinical safety data. [8]

## How should BPC-157 pens and vials be stored?

Keep BPC-157 pens and liquid vials refrigerated at 2-8 °C (36-46 °F), protected from light, and never frozen. The seller lists a practical shelf life of 4-6 weeks for pens and 16-20 weeks for liquid vials. [20]

- Temperature: 2-8 °C (36-46 °F). Avoid long periods at room temperature.
- Freezing: do not freeze either liquid format.
- Light and moisture: store in the original packaging, away from light.
- Discard: after the seller's shelf-life window, or sooner if the solution turns cloudy, discolored, or shows particles.
- Records: note the lot number and the date the pen or vial was first opened.

## Is BPC-157 legal in the U.S.? Regulatory status as of November 2026

BPC-157 is not FDA-approved for any use. It is sold in the U.S. labeled for laboratory research use only. In 2023 FDA flagged it as a compounding substance that may present significant safety risks; in July 2026 an FDA advisory committee voted 8-6 to recommend reversing that, but FDA has not finalized any change.

- 2023: FDA places BPC-157 among bulk drug substances for compounding that may present significant safety risks, citing possible immune reactions, peptide impurities, and limited safety information. [13]
- July 23, 2026: FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee votes 8 to 6, with one abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157 to the list of substances compounding pharmacies may use. FDA's own review staff had recommended against it. [14, 15]
- Now: the vote is advisory. Any change requires FDA rulemaking, which has not been completed. BPC-157 remains unapproved. [14, 15]

Sport and military: the World Anti-Doping Agency lists BPC-157 under S0, non-approved substances, prohibited at all times. [16] The U.S. Department of Defense also lists it as prohibited for service members. [17]

"Research use only" is a label for materials intended for laboratory work. It is not an FDA approval, and it does not make a product a drug, a dietary supplement, or suitable for human use. This is general information, not legal advice.

## What are the open safety questions about BPC-157?

The main open questions are the lack of human safety data, a theoretical concern that encouraging blood-vessel growth could also support tumor growth, and the quality of products sold online. [8, 18, 19]

- No clinical safety data: reviewers found no controlled data on safety in people. [8]
- Blood-vessel growth: the pro-angiogenic effect seen in lab studies has raised a theoretical concern about tumor growth that has not been ruled in or out. [6, 18]
- Product quality: peptides sold online may not contain what the label says, which is why lot-specific testing matters. [19]
- Unknown long-term effects: no long-term studies exist at scale. [9]
