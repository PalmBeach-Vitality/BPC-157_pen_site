Anatomy of each format

The pen keeps BPC-157 sealed in a cartridge and dispenses through its dial. The vial is a glass container with a rubber stopper under a crimped seal and a blue cap.

Side by side

Choose the pen for the fewest handling steps. Choose the vial for more volume and a longer listed shelf life. Freeze-dried BPC-157 is powder that must be reconstituted first; Palm Beach Vitality offers it for bulk orders only.

What a BPC-157 pen is

A BPC-157 pen holds the peptide already dissolved in a sealed 3 mL cartridge, with a dial for dispensing measured volumes in the lab. Each pen is labeled with its total peptide content and lot number.1

Pens are newer for research peptides than vials. Their appeal in the lab is fewer handling steps: no transfer from a vial, no separate measuring tool, and a cartridge that stays sealed between uses. The trade-off is a smaller volume and a shorter refrigerated shelf life.

The 10 mg and 20 mg pens use the same 3 mL cartridge. The 20 mg pen holds twice the peptide, so its concentration is about 6.67 mg/mL instead of about 3.33 mg/mL.

Vial sizes

Palm Beach Vitality uses blue-capped glass vials in 2 mL, 5 mL and 10 mL sizes. BPC-157 liquid vials are the 10 mL size.1
