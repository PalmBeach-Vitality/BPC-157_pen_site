What is KLOW?

KLOW is the name Palm Beach Vitality uses for a solution of four peptides: KPV, BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu. Both the pen and the vial are labeled 10 mg, 10 mg, 10 mg, and 50 mg. The blend is not one 15-residue chain, and KLOW as sold is not an FDA-approved drug.1,2

How strong is the evidence?

There is no published study of the blend. The papers above are about one peptide at a time. A paper about one peptide is not a paper about four peptides in a pen.10

  1. Cell studiesNone of this pen or vial. One component paper studied KPV in cells.5
  2. Animal studiesNone of this pen or vial. Component papers used mice, and one paper reviews animal models of BPC-157 alone.5–7
  3. Human studiesNo published study of this four-peptide blend in people.
  4. Controlled human trialsNone published.

This site does not turn a component paper into a claim about KLOW. The research page keeps each paper with the peptide it actually studied.

Open safety questions

  • No study of the combination. There is no published study of this four-peptide pen or vial as one product.10
  • Not an approved drug. KLOW as sold is not an FDA-approved drug. It is labeled for laboratory research use only.1
  • Label versus this lot. The Accumark certificate for lot KL102126 measures 77.046 mg against 80.0 mg declared. Those figures are that lot. They are not a rewrite of the label.4
  • Not a use protocol. This site does not give dosing, click-count instructions for use, or a reconstitution guide. Both catalog formats are already in aqueous solution.
