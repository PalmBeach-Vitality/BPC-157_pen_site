How KLOW is made.

Each of the four peptides is made by the same general solid-phase method. The four are then combined in aqueous solution and filled. The pen and the vial ship already dissolved. This page stays at that level. It is not a synthesis recipe, and it does not give conditions, reagents, or a protocol.1,2

The short version

KLOW is not extracted from tissue as a blend. The peptides are made chemically, one component at a time, then combined.

The bench photographs are illustrative lab scenes. They do not show a specific manufacturer’s facility. The pen and vial photograph later on this page is the seller’s product, not a factory.

The general method

Solid-phase peptide synthesis builds a chain on a tiny resin bead, one amino acid at a time. Bruce Merrifield introduced the method in 1963. It is now automated.1

The growing chain stays attached to the bead, so leftover reagents can be washed away between additions. When the chain is finished, it is released, and chromatography separates the intended peptide from shorter or flawed chains. That is the general method used for peptide components of this kind. This page does not specify protecting groups, solvents, temperatures, or quantities.1,2

GHK-Cu is the copper complex of the glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine tripeptide. The complex is a description of that component. This page does not describe how the copper is added.

From chains to a fill

  1. 01

    Building blocks

    Start from the amino acids

    Each peptide starts from its own amino acids. The four chains are not one 15-residue sequence. The photograph is an illustrative bench, not a specific facility.

  2. 02

    Separate chains

    Make each peptide

    KPV, BPC-157, the TB-500 fragment, and GHK are each assembled as their own chain. They are not grown as one 15-residue sequence.

  3. 03

    Release

    Take each chain off the resin

    The finished chain is cut free of the bead. What remains is crude peptide, still to be separated from the mistakes of synthesis.

  4. 04

    Purification

    Separate, then identify

    HPLC separates the intended peptide from shorter chains. A later test checks that the material is the peptide named on the label. The KLOW pen’s own chromatogram is on the testing page. The picture below is not that chart.

  5. 05

    Combine

    Put the four in solution

    The purified components are combined in aqueous solution. The seller describes that solution as an acetate salt form. The pen and the vial are filled from solution. They do not ship as dry powder.

  6. 06

    Bulk powder

    Freeze-drying is a different order

    The seller offers freeze-dried KLOW as a bulk order. The vial page says to make contact, and that a minimum applies. The pen and the liquid vial are not that powder. This site does not give a reconstitution guide.3

  7. 07

    The catalog

    Fill the pen or the vial

    The solution is filled into a 3 mL pre-filled pen or a 10 mL vial and labeled with a lot number. The photograph is the seller’s picture of the pen.
