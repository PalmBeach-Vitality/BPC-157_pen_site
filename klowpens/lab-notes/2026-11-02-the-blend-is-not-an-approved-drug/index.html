The blend is not an approved drug

KLOW as sold is not an FDA-approved drug, and it is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. One component, BPC-157, has a separate regulatory record. That record describes the component. It does not assign a status to the name KLOW.

By the klowpens.com Editorial Team · November 2, 2026

The component with its own page

BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. In 2023 FDA listed it among compounding substances that may present significant safety risks. A July 2026 advisory committee vote recommended a change, and FDA has not finalized that change. The World Anti-Doping Agency prohibits BPC-157 under S0. Those sentences are documented on bpc157pens.com. They are about BPC-157.

This site does not say the word KLOW appears on the WADA list. It also does not invent a compounding category for the four-peptide blend. A research-use label on the pen or the vial is not an approval.

What this note refuses to do

It does not give a dose, a click count for use, or a way to reconstitute a dry blend. Format specifications, including the pen’s 300 clicks at 0.01 mL per click, stay on the comparison page as specifications.
