The label and the certificate are different sentences

The KLOW label states four amounts that add to 80 mg. The certificate for pen lot KL102126 measured 77.046 mg in that lot. Those are different sentences. One is what the seller says is in the product. The other is what one laboratory reported for one sample.

By the klowpens.com Editorial Team · October 5, 2026

Why they are not required to match on the digit

A label is the specification the product is sold under: 10 mg KPV, 10 mg BPC-157, 10 mg TB-500, 50 mg GHK-Cu. A certificate is an analytical result. For this lot the measured amounts were KPV 11.039 mg, BPC-157 9.688 mg, TB-500 (17–23 fragment) 11.1 mg, and GHK-Cu 45.219 mg. They sit near the label. They are not the label copied into another typeface.

Treating the certificate as a corrected label would erase the lot. The next pen is not sample 8V4W-UP8E unless the paperwork says it is. Match the lot number on the container to the lot number on the report before using the measured figures at all.

What neither sentence is

Neither the label nor the certificate is a study of the blend, an approval, or a direction to dispense a volume. Purity near 99% on an analyte is a chromatography result for that analyte in that sample.
