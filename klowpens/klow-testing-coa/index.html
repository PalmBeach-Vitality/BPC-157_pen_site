What to check

On this certificate, check the four names, the lot, the purity, the identity lines, and who signed it.

  1. Four names. The declared identity is KPV, GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB500 (17–23 fragment). Not one sequence.
  2. Lot number. KL102126 on this certificate. A different pen or vial needs the lot printed on its own label.
  3. HPLC purity. The blend result is 99.78%, against a specification of at least 98%. Page 2 gives a purity for each peptide.
  4. Identity. Each named analyte is marked “conforms.” These three pages do not print a mass. This site does not invent one.
  5. Who signed. Anthony Burke, PhD, reviewed it. Levi Fried, MD, certified it. Published October 26, 2026.

Supplier red flags

Be wary of a research-peptide seller that makes health claims, gives dosing advice, or cannot produce a lot-specific certificate. Products sold online may not contain what the label says.3

  • Health or healing claims for a research-use-only product.
  • Dosing charts, click-count instructions, or a reconstitution guide presented as use.
  • Before-and-after photos or testimonials.
  • A certificate with no lot number, no lab name, or no date.
  • One generic certificate used for every batch.
